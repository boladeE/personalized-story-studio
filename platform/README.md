# Little Things Digital platform (branch `platform_v1`)

End to end, for nine occasions: a sender answers six short screens, watches the film, adjusts it, locks it with a question, and gets a private link. The recipient answers the question and plays the gift. The sender can delete it. Payment isn't connected: gifts are created in test mode and nothing is charged.

    npm install
    cp .env.example .env.local   # optional: add GEMINI_API_KEY for AI songs (Lyria 3.5)
    npm run dev                  # http://localhost:3000
    npm test                     # unit tests (Vitest)
    npm run test:e2e             # browser tests (Playwright, /usr/bin/google-chrome; songs use the demo provider)
    npm run build && npm start   # production server (srvx), reads .env.local if present
    E2E_BASE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:3000 npm run test:e2e   # run the browser tests against a running server

## Flow

1. `/`: the landing page (`src/routes/index.tsx`, `src/styles/home.css`), designed as the front of a small cinema: a hibiscus field with the headline set at poster scale, and the sample film on a marquee-lit screen that hangs over the field's lower edge. In order: that hero with the real film player showing the fictional sample; the occasions as a rack of tickets, each linking to `/create?occasion=<id>`; what goes into a film, as eight stills from the sample (the same `Still` component the writing flow uses); the song, showing one memory the fictional sender wrote beside the lines the song made of it, with a player for the sample's song (`public/sample/zara-song.mp3`, Lyria 3.5); four steps; questions; a closing call to action. The film and the song player take turns: starting one pauses the other (`FILM_SOUND` and `FILM_HUSH` window events in `Film.tsx`). The sample is marked "A fictional sample" under the player, the footer and the sample's end credits say Zara and Tobi are fictional, and the preview header carries only controls. The page states no price: the cost question says payment isn't connected and films are free during testing. It has no reviews, counts or guarantees, because there are none to report.

   Occasions live in `src/story/occasions.ts`: birthday, anniversary, Valentine's Day, just because, Mother's Day, Father's Day, thank you, congratulations, missing you. An occasion changes the ticket's corner, the film's opening line, a few template lines, the questions the form asks for the reasons and the last line, the ending (a birthday ends on a candle to blow out; every other occasion ends on a sealed note to open), the suggested share message, and what the lyric writer and music model are told the song is for. The scenes and their order are shared. Birthday output is unchanged from before occasions existed, and gifts stored before then play as birthdays.
2. `/start`: the short flow, and where every "Start a film" button and occasion ticket leads (`src/routes/start.tsx`, `src/styles/start.css`). It is laid out as a box-office counter: on a dark green side the film's ticket is made out as the sender answers (their name, who it is from, the occasion), and their photos and first words gather under it; on the bright side there is one question at a time. On a phone the counter is a band across the top. Six screens: who it is for (13 relationships, a tap moves on), both names, the occasion (a tap; skipped when the visitor came from a ticket), photos (optional), one box of the sender's own words (30 characters or more), and the song's style and voice (skipped when songs are off). "Make the film" then does three things:
   - **Sorts the words.** `POST /api/sort` sends the box and the two names to a Google text model (`gemini-3.8-flash`, low thinking, about 5 to 7 seconds; `GEMINI_SORT_MODEL`), which is briefed to sort, not write: memories with a title lifted from their words, habits, reasons, a spotlight line and a last line (`src/server/sort.ts`). Every piece that comes back is checked against the sender's text (`keepTheirWords` in `src/story/notes.ts`) on the server and again in the browser, and anything containing words they didn't write is dropped. If the model is off, slow, wrong or over its hourly cap (`SORT_HOURLY_PER_CLIENT`, 30), the text is sorted by rule instead (`sortLocally`), so the film is always built. In three real notes the model added nothing and moved every sentence sensibly.
   - **Builds the film** and goes to the preview at once. Scene minimums were lowered so a few sentences are enough: one memory (told by itself, without the "1 of n" card), two habits, two reasons. A lone reason or habit becomes the line beside their photo rather than being lost.
   - **Starts the song** (`startSong` in `src/components/draft.tsx`): the words are written, then sung in the chosen style and voice ("Surprise me" uses the Afrobeats recipe). A strip under the preview header says where it has got to, and the film is held at its opening until the song is ready (see Music). There is no way to make a film without its song.

   The relationship (`src/story/relationships.ts`) is optional in the data. It tells the lyric writer who they are to each other, makes the music a love song only for a partner, and keeps "Staying anyway" out of the red flags for anyone else.
3. `/create`: the detailed form, where each scene is written by hand. It is reached from the short flow's first screen and from the preview's editor, where each scene links to its step. The first screen asks for both names and the occasion (preselected when the visitor came from a ticket; `?occasion=` is written into the draft and then dropped from the address). One question per screen (`?step=`), with a still of the scene it feeds beside it, filled with the sender's words as they type (`src/film/Still.tsx`, `src/film/previews.ts`). A strip of frames shows which scenes have enough to be in the film and jumps to any step. The draft stays in the tab (photos are `blob:` URLs) until it is sent.

   **Photos** (`src/components/photos.ts`, `photo-intake.ts`): up to 12, 15 MB each. iPhone photos are HEIC, which only Safari can show, so a HEIC file is redrawn as a JPEG on the sender's device when it is added (recognised by its type, its name or its first bytes, since a computer often gives it no type). Safari does this itself; other browsers fetch a decoder (`heic-to`, a build of libheif, LGPL-3.0, about 0.8 MB compressed) the first time one is needed. Nothing is uploaded to do it. A file that isn't a photo, or can't be read, is named in a message. Video clips are not supported.
4. `/preview`: the generated film, under a light header. It is a page for watching: the header has two links, **Edit** and **Send this gift** (the sample has Edit and Start yours), and the film's credits end with "Send this gift", "Change something" and "Watch it again". The sample (`?sample=1`, and `&style=scrapbook` to open it as an album) opens as a Screening in the light Paper look, and can be changed like a sender's.
   - **Edit** (`?edit=1`, so Back leaves it, and Done does too) puts a desk under the film with three tabs. *Scenes* is a row of stills in playing order, then what was left out and what could still go in; choosing one offers, in its card, its words first (a link to its step of the detailed form), then another design, move earlier or later, leave out or put back. The last change can be undone from the same card. *Look* picks the kind of film and its colours without redrawing anything else, and "Try another version" is a new seed: it redraws anything the sender has not chosen, including the kind of film and each scene's design ("Back to the last version" returns to the one before). *Song* shows the film's song and its words, switches between takes when there is more than one, and links to the song page.
   - While editing, the stage is the film only while it plays; otherwise it is a still of the chosen scene with "Play from here" (`FilmHandle.playFrom`, called from that tap so sound is allowed). A film that is playing goes to the scene that is chosen or changed, with the music still playing. While the song is being made the stills can be looked through and the scenes changed, and only playing waits.
5. `/song`: the song, for changing the words or the tune by hand. The studio writes the words from the occasion and the sender's name, reasons, habits, memories and last line (`POST /api/lyrics`, a Google text model); the sender edits them on a ruled sheet, picks one of five tunes after hearing a 16-second sample of each (`public/tunes/`), and hears it sung (`POST /api/music` with `lyrics`, about a minute). Each version is kept as a take for the sitting and the film plays the chosen one. "Music only" makes an instrumental and sends nothing personal. The page says once, plainly, that Google's AI models write and sing it and what is sent.
6. `/send`: question and answer for the lock, then "Create the private link". Photos are redrawn as JPEGs (max 2000px, no EXIF or GPS) in the browser before upload.
7. `/manage/<id>#k=<key>`: the sender's page with the link, a message to copy, and delete. The key stays in the URL fragment, so it isn't sent in requests or referrers. Only its hash is stored.
8. `/g/<id>`: the recipient. Nothing about the gift shows until the answer is right. Answers ignore case, spacing and edge punctuation, and are stored as scrypt hashes. A correct answer sets an HttpOnly signed cookie for 30 days. Photos and the song are served only with that cookie. Guesses are limited to 10 per gift per 15 minutes.

## Kinds of film, designs and the sender's cut

No two films should look the same, and the sender should not have to work for that. Three layers do it:

- A **world** is a kind of film: a design family with its own opening, pages, type and motion. There are two, `screening` (a private cinema) and `scrapbook` (a handmade album). They are listed in `src/story/worlds.ts` with the names of their three colourways.
- Each scene type can have several **designs** in a world, listed in the same file. In the Scrapbook the main character is a polaroid or a print in photo corners, memories are taped prints or cards with the photo clipped on, red flags are sticky notes or a ticked checklist, reasons are luggage tags on a string or index cards, and the camera roll is loose prints or a contact sheet. In the Screening the red flags are loud cards or a charge sheet that builds, and the reasons are big numerals or a running list.
- The **cut** is what the sender changed (`Intake.cut`): the order of scenes, scenes left out, and the design chosen for a scene. The opening and the ending cannot be moved or dropped.

`generateStory` records the world on the story and the design on each scene. Anything the sender has not chosen is drawn from the seed, from a second random stream, so adding a design or a world never changes which words or photos a seed picks. Scenes are built in the studio's order and then rearranged, so moving or dropping one does not change another. A gift stores its finished story, so it keeps its world, designs and cut; gifts stored before worlds existed play as Screenings. To add a design: list it in `worlds.ts`, then draw it in `shots.tsx` (Screening) or `scrapbook.tsx` (Scrapbook) by checking `shot.design`. To add a world: add it to `WORLD_IDS` and `WORLDS`, give it palettes in `looks.ts`, a shot renderer and an opening, and a stylesheet.

## Music

A film's only music is the song written and sung for it. There is no built-in or studio music anywhere: none can be chosen, none plays while a song is being made, and none stands in when a song fails. The synth score that used to do those things has been removed from the engine, the generator, the story format and the audio code (stored test gifts that named score tracks simply play without them).

- While its song is being made, the film is held at its opening (the ticket or the album cover, dimmed, with "It starts when their song is ready") and cannot be started. The sender can change scenes and style meanwhile.
- When the song arrives it goes onto the film by itself and the film can be played.
- If the song fails, or songs are switched off in this studio, the film plays in silence, the credits carry no Music line, and the send page says the film has no song.
- A song is never asked for twice at once (`inFlight` in `src/components/draft.tsx`).
- Google's music model refuses lyrics that come too close to an existing song (seen live on a thank-you song for a friend). When that happens the words are written again, with a note to the writer to keep clear of song titles and famous lines, and sung again, up to three times, before the sender is told anything.

## The film (`src/film/`)

The gift is a film that plays by itself, not a slideshow.

- `timeline.ts`: turns a story into timed shots. Lengths come from reading time, snapped to whole bars of the scene's music so cuts land on the beat. Plain data, so it can later drive an MP4 render.
- `Film.tsx`: the player. Tapping the ticket tears it, a film leader counts 3-2-1, then a clock runs the shots. The film waits at the candle (or, for anything but a birthday, the sealed note) and at the end. There is a player bar (back, pause, next, scrubber with scene marks, sound, full screen) and keyboard control (Space, arrows, M, F). It pauses when the tab is hidden.
- `shots.tsx`: what each shot looks like: lines typed letter by letter, the name drawn from dust, scene cards, framed photos with a slow push, flat-colour red flags that slam in on the beat, prints dealt for the camera roll, a letter that scrolls as it is read, the candle or the sealed note, the last line and rolling credits.
- `backdrop.ts`: WebGL smoke behind everything. It drifts between palettes per mood. Falls back to a CSS gradient.
- `particles.ts`: dust, words that gather from or scatter into dust, candle sparks and confetti.
- `scrapbook.tsx` and `src/styles/scrapbook.css`: the Scrapbook. The same shots drawn as pages of an album: a closed book that opens, one title page, chapter pages with a band of tape, prints with tape, corners and clips, handwriting (Caveat) and typed slips (Courier Prime, both under the Open Font License in `public/fonts`). The page turns between shots; a list (sticky notes, checklist, tags, cards) builds on the page it is on. No smoke, dust or letterbox bars.
- `looks.ts`: the three colourways of each world as palettes.
- Reduced motion: no countdown, particles or smoke movement; shots still change with simple fades.
- Styles: `src/styles/film.css` (sized in container units, so the film scales to its box) and `src/styles/room.css` (the studio's working pages: a hibiscus field with white cards over a bright wall, the same language as the home page).

## Server

- `src/routes/api/*`: plain Request/Response handlers (create, unlock, gift, media, manage, music, song).
- `src/server/store.ts`: one folder per gift under `DATA_DIR` (`gift.json` and `media/`). It can be swapped for Postgres and R2 later behind the same functions.
- `src/story/validate.ts`: server-side limits on every intake field. The server rebuilds the story from the intake and seed, so it never trusts a story sent by the browser.
- `src/server/music.ts`: Lyria 3.5 through the Gemini Interactions API (preferred), or legacy Lyria 3 Pro through OpenRouter (Lyria 3.5 isn't on OpenRouter yet). Songs carry Google's inaudible SynthID watermark. Sung prompts (`sungPrompt` in `src/story/vibes.ts`) carry the recipient's and sender's names and up to three reasons, three habits, two memories and the wish, cleaned and capped on the server; the letter and photos are never sent. Instrumental prompts carry nothing personal. When the request carries `lyrics` (the normal path), `lyricsPrompt` asks for the song as a recipe in the order Google's Lyria guidance recommends: genre and tempo, instruments, mood, the vocalist (`voice`: a man's, a woman's, or left open), production, structure, then the lyrics to sing exactly. This is the shape that produced the sample's song, and the Afrobeats style is that song's recipe. The other four styles were rewritten into the same shape and have not been listened to in it. In the one real comparison made with the earlier prompt, all 22 lines came back word for word with nothing added. The approved words and the song's title are stored with it. Google refuses a request whose lyrics quote an existing song (HTTP 400, code `recitation`); the line "Happy birthday to you" does this, so it is caught in the page, in the endpoint (422) and in the writer's output. There are per-client and daily caps. `MUSIC_PROVIDER=demo` makes short local tones for tests.
- `src/components/audio-engine.ts`: a song is streamed through a media element wired into Web Audio, so it starts as soon as the first second has arrived (measured at 1.5 Mbit/s: the old download-then-decode left about 12 seconds of silence after the film began; streaming starts with the film). The element is played once, muted, inside the tap so gesture-only browsers allow it later; if streaming is refused, the whole file is decoded as before. Songs are served with byte ranges (`mediaResponse` in `src/server/store.ts`).
- `src/server/lyrics.ts`: writes the words with `gemini-pro-latest` (`GEMINI_LYRICS_MODEL`), low thinking, JSON output, about 15 to 25 seconds. The brief (`lyricsBrief` in `src/story/vibes.ts`) has two tones, chosen on the song page. `smooth` (the default) writes a spare track on one hook from the sender's own phrase, uses at most three details without explaining them, and keeps the name and "happy birthday" for the bridge and outro. `story` tells one moment in order, teases with the punchline last and puts the birthday in the chorus. Each carries a worked example about two invented friends, kept free of any one country's references. For other occasions the same brief swaps in that occasion's greeting (or none, for "just because") and adds a line saying the worked example is a birthday song and this one is not. Only the birthday brief has been tuned by ear; for the other occasions one set of lyrics each was written and read for anniversary, just because and missing you (right greeting, in the bridge and outro only, no birthday wording), but no song has been made or heard for them. In side-by-side reads, Flash at the same speed invented details to reach rhymes, and higher thinking levels took two minutes without writing better. Same key as the music. Caps: `LYRICS_HOURLY_PER_CLIENT` (20) and `LYRICS_DAILY_LIMIT` (300). `MUSIC_PROVIDER=demo` returns placeholder words.
- Production needs `SESSION_SECRET` (32+ characters). Without it, the server refuses to unlock gifts.

## Not done yet

- Real payment (Paystack) and emailed receipts. Today the sender must bookmark the manage page.
- Lyria 3.5 through the Gemini API has made real songs (about two minutes, MP3, 40 to 55 seconds to make). The OpenRouter path has never been called with a real key. Gemini keys must now be bound to a service account in Google Cloud, and the project needs prepaid credit.
- Two kinds of film so far. The scene editor changes designs, order and which scenes are in; it does not yet edit words or swap photos in place (it links to the detailed form for words), and scenes can't be repeated or added beyond the nine types. The detailed form's stills and the home page's scene stills are drawn as a Screening.
- While a song is being made the preview shows a strip under the header (what is happening and three steps); its bars are timed estimates, not measured progress.
- Small interaction rules, taken from a reading of Emil Kowalski's "apple-design" skill: anything pressable answers on press (a slight shrink); removing something is never confirmed first but can be undone (a photo, the whole draft via Clear or Fill with the sample, the last change in the scene editor), with only deleting a sent gift still asking; the send form says what is wrong beside the field once the sender has been in it, and refuses a question that contains its own answer; the film's opening can be skipped with a tap or a key; the film's control bar goes solid for people who ask for less transparency or more contrast.
- Photo tagging, per-line edits, swapping photos. No email is collected, so the sender still has to keep their manage link.
- Every film has a song made for it (about $0.08 each plus the words): the short flow starts one when the film is made, and a film built from the detailed form starts one when it reaches the preview. Past the daily and hourly caps the song fails, the preview says so with a Try again, and the film is silent until it is made.
- The film has only been watched in headless Chrome captures and one real Chrome window. It has not been checked on real phones, Safari or Firefox, and nobody has listened to it.
- AI songs don't have a beat map, so cuts follow a default tempo rather than the song.
- Sung songs: nobody has checked how Lyria pronounces Nigerian names. Written lyrics are a first draft: the writer still pads the odd line to make a rhyme, which is why the sender edits before the music is made. Takes live only in the tab; a reload loses them. Lyric writing needs `GEMINI_API_KEY` even when music goes through OpenRouter.
- More occasions (wedding, apology, memorial would each need their own wording, and a memorial its own tone), MP4 film export, accounts.
- Songs for occasions other than a birthday have not been made or listened to.
- Clean-up of unused draft songs, and backups of `DATA_DIR`.
- Railway: needs a service on this branch with a volume mounted at `DATA_DIR`, `SESSION_SECRET` set, build `npm run build` and start `npm start`.
