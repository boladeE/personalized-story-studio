# Little Things Digital platform (branch `platform_v1`)

End to end, for nine occasions: a sender answers six short screens, watches the film, adjusts it, locks it with a question, and gets a private link. The recipient answers the question and plays the gift. The sender can delete it. Payment isn't connected: gifts are created in test mode and nothing is charged.

    npm install
    cp .env.example .env.local   # optional: add GEMINI_API_KEY for AI songs (Lyria 3.5)
    npm run dev                  # http://localhost:3000
    npm test                     # unit tests (Vitest)
    npm run test:e2e             # browser tests (Playwright, /usr/bin/google-chrome; songs use the demo provider)
    npm run build && npm start   # production server (srvx), reads .env.local if present
    E2E_BASE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:3000 npm run test:e2e   # run the browser tests against a running server

## Flow

1. `/`: the landing page (`src/routes/index.tsx`, `src/styles/home.css`), designed as the front of a small cinema: a pale mist field with the headline set at poster scale in deep green (hibiscus is kept for the buttons), and the sample film on a marquee-lit screen that hangs over the field's lower edge. In order: that hero with the real film player showing the fictional sample; the occasions as a rack of tickets, each linking to `/create?occasion=<id>`; what goes into a film, as eight stills from the sample (the same `Still` component the writing flow uses); the song, showing one memory the fictional sender wrote beside the lines the song made of it, with a player for the sample's song (`public/sample/zara-song.mp3`, Lyria 3.5); four steps; questions; a closing call to action. The film and the song player take turns: starting one pauses the other (`FILM_SOUND` and `FILM_HUSH` window events in `Film.tsx`). The sample is marked "A fictional sample" under the player, the footer and the sample's end credits say Zara and Tobi are fictional, and the preview header carries only controls. The page states no price: the cost question says payment isn't connected and films are free during testing. It has no reviews, counts or guarantees, because there are none to report.

   Occasions live in `src/story/occasions.ts`: birthday, anniversary, Valentine's Day, just because, Mother's Day, Father's Day, thank you, congratulations, missing you. An occasion changes the ticket's corner, the film's opening line, a few template lines, the questions the form asks for the reasons and the last line, the ending (a birthday ends on a candle to blow out; every other occasion ends on a sealed note to open), the suggested share message, and what the lyric writer and music model are told the song is for. The scenes and their order are shared. Birthday output is unchanged from before occasions existed, and gifts stored before then play as birthdays.
2. `/start`: the short flow, and where every "Start a film" button and occasion ticket leads (`src/routes/start.tsx`, `src/styles/start.css`). It is laid out as a box-office counter: on a dark green side the film's ticket is made out as the sender answers (their name, who it is from, the occasion), and their photos and first words gather under it; on the bright side there is one question at a time. On a phone the counter is a band across the top. Six screens: who it is for (13 relationships, a tap moves on), both names, the occasion (a tap; skipped when the visitor came from a ticket), photos (optional), one box of the sender's own words (30 characters or more), and the song's style and voice (skipped when songs are off). "Make the film" then does three things:
   - **Writes the film's scenes.** `POST /api/sort` sends the box, the two names, the occasion and any follow-up answers to a Google text model, which outlines the film: its own scenes, named, with the sender's words placed in them (see "a film's scenes are its own" below). Every line that comes back is checked against the sender's text on the server and again in the browser, and anything containing words they didn't write is dropped. If the model is off, slow, wrong or over its hourly cap (`SORT_HOURLY_PER_CLIENT`, 30), the note is outlined by rule instead, so the film is always built.
   - **Builds the film** and goes to the preview at once. Scene minimums were lowered so a few sentences are enough: one memory (told by itself, without the "1 of n" card), two habits, two reasons. A lone reason or habit becomes the line beside their photo rather than being lost.
   - **Starts the song** (`startSong` in `src/components/draft.tsx`): the words are written, then sung in the chosen style and voice ("Surprise me" uses the Afrobeats recipe). A strip under the preview header says where it has got to, and the film is held at its opening until the song is ready (see Music). There is no way to make a film without its song.

   The relationship (`src/story/relationships.ts`) is optional in the data. It tells the lyric writer who they are to each other, makes the music a love song only for a partner, and keeps "Staying anyway" out of the red flags for anyone else.
3. `/create`: the detailed form, where each scene is written by hand. It is reached from the short flow's first screen and from the preview's editor ("Edit every detail"). The first screen asks for both names and the occasion (preselected when the visitor came from a ticket; `?occasion=` is written into the draft and then dropped from the address). One question per screen (`?step=`), with a still of the scene it feeds beside it, filled with the sender's words as they type (`src/film/Still.tsx`, `src/film/previews.ts`). A strip of frames shows which scenes have enough to be in the film and jumps to any step. The draft stays in the tab (photos are `blob:` URLs) until it is sent.

   **Photos** (`src/components/photos.ts`, `photo-intake.ts`): up to 12, 15 MB each. iPhone photos are HEIC, which only Safari can show, so a HEIC file is redrawn as a JPEG on the sender's device when it is added (recognised by its type, its name or its first bytes, since a computer often gives it no type). Safari does this itself; other browsers fetch a decoder (`heic-to`, a build of libheif, LGPL-3.0, about 0.8 MB compressed) the first time one is needed. Nothing is uploaded to do it. A file that isn't a photo, or can't be read, is named in a message. Video clips are not supported.
4. `/preview`: the generated film, under a light header. It is a page for watching: the header has two links, **Edit** and **Send this gift** (the sample has Edit and Start yours), and the film's credits end with "Send this gift", "Change something" and "Watch it again". The sample (`?sample=1`, and `&style=scrapbook` to open it as an album) opens as a Screening in the light Paper look, and can be changed like a sender's.
   - **Edit** (`?edit=1`, so Back leaves it, and Done does too) puts the editor around the film, with no tabs (`src/components/editor.tsx`). The chosen scene is a panel beside the still (under it on a phone or tablet, where the editor is a scrolling page with the still kept at the top): its words are open in place the moment the scene is chosen (`src/components/SceneWords.tsx`, the same fields as the detailed form, from `src/components/fields.tsx`), and under them are another design, move earlier or later, leave out or put back. The still follows the line being written, and while a field has the cursor the film is rebuilt a moment behind the typing (`useDeferredValue`). Under the still is the row of scenes in playing order, then what was left out and what could still go in, with **Look** (the kind of film and its colours, shown in the same panel) and **Try another version** (a new seed: it redraws anything the sender has not chosen) at its head. **Undo**, beside Done, takes back the editor's changes one step at a time (words, designs, order, look, version); a removed photo has its own undo. Changing a scene's words does not remake the song.
   - While editing, the stage is the film only while it plays; otherwise it is a still of the chosen scene with "Play from here" (`FilmHandle.playFrom`, called from that tap so sound is allowed) and, while it plays, "Stop". A film that is playing goes to the scene that is chosen or changed, with the music still playing, and stops when a field is typed in (`FilmHandle.rewind`). While the song is being made the stills can be looked through and the scenes changed, and only playing waits.
   - The film's song has a bar of its own at the foot of the editor, which opens the song's room (`?edit=1&song=1`, `src/components/SongRoom.tsx`): the song played by itself from one button (the film is rewound first), its takes when there is more than one, and its words laid out in their parts (`lyricParts` in `src/story/vibes.ts`). "Want it different?" offers three ways to record it again: the same words sung afresh, another of the five tunes after hearing a 16-second sample of each (`public/tunes/`, with "Music only" for an instrumental, which sends nothing personal), or other words, edited on the sheet or written again by the studio (`POST /api/lyrics`). Words changed on the sheet are also what a new tune sings. Each makes a new take (`remakeSong` in `src/components/draft.tsx`, `POST /api/music`, about a minute and a half) while the film keeps the song it has; the take the sender picks is the one the film plays. The room says once, plainly, that Google's AI models write and sing it and what is sent.
5. `/song`: the song's old address. It now redirects to the song's room in the editor.
6. `/send`: question and answer for the lock, then "Create the private link". Photos are redrawn as JPEGs (max 2000px, no EXIF or GPS) in the browser before upload.
7. `/manage/<id>#k=<key>`: the sender's page with the link, a message to copy, and delete. The key stays in the URL fragment, so it isn't sent in requests or referrers. Only its hash is stored.
8. `/g/<id>`: the recipient. Nothing about the gift shows until the answer is right. Answers ignore case, spacing and edge punctuation, and are stored as scrypt hashes. A correct answer sets an HttpOnly signed cookie for 30 days. Photos and the song are served only with that cookie. Guesses are limited to 10 per gift per 15 minutes.

## Kinds of film, designs and the sender's cut

No two films should look the same, and the sender should not have to work for that. Three layers do it:

- A **world** is a kind of film: a design family with its own opening, pages, type and motion. There are three: `screening` (a private cinema), `scrapbook` (a handmade album) and `mixtape` (a tape made for one person, on the `platform_v2` branch). They are listed in `src/story/worlds.ts` with the names of their three colourways.
- Each scene type can have several **designs** in a world, listed in the same file. In the Scrapbook the main character is a polaroid or a print in photo corners, memories are taped prints or cards with the photo clipped on, red flags are sticky notes or a ticked checklist, reasons are luggage tags on a string or index cards, and the camera roll is loose prints or a contact sheet. In the Screening the red flags are loud cards or a charge sheet that builds, and the reasons are big numerals or a running list.
- The **cut** is what the sender changed (`Intake.cut`): the order of scenes, scenes left out, and the design chosen for a scene. The opening and the ending cannot be moved or dropped.

`generateStory` records the world on the story and the design on each scene. Anything the sender has not chosen is drawn from the seed, from a second random stream, so adding a design or a world never changes which words or photos a seed picks. Scenes are built in the studio's order and then rearranged, so moving or dropping one does not change another. A gift stores its finished story, so it keeps its world, designs and cut; gifts stored before worlds existed play as Screenings. To add a design: list it in `worlds.ts`, then draw it in `shots.tsx` (Screening) or `scrapbook.tsx` (Scrapbook) by checking `shot.design`. To add a world: add it to `WORLD_IDS` and `WORLDS`, give it palettes in `looks.ts`, a shot renderer and an opening, and a stylesheet.

On `platform_v2` the short flow has a step between the sender's note and the song: **follow-up questions**. The studio reads the note and asks up to three short questions, each quoting a part of it (`POST /api/followups`, `src/server/followups.ts`, the same text model; `followupsLocally` in `src/story/followups.ts` asks plainer ones by rule when the model is off, slow or wrong). Every question is checked to hang on words the sender wrote (`cleanFollowups`), and the step is skipped when there is nothing to ask. Answers are optional. They are the sender's own words, so they are sent with the note to be sorted (`answers` on `POST /api/sort`), kept with the part they were about, and count as "their words" in the check that guards the film (`withAnswers`). The questions themselves never appear in the film.

On `platform_v2` the sender chooses **how the film ends** (a step of the short flow, after the occasion). Unset, it ends the way its occasion does: a candle to blow out, or a sealed note. Or it ends on the sender's own question (the viewer answers "Yes" or "Obviously") or invitation (a plan with two things to pick from). Either way the film holds until the viewer answers, then shows the sender's last line; after an invitation the reveal also says what was picked. It is stored as `Intake.ending` (`EndingChoice` in `src/story/types.ts`; `endingOf` returns it only when it is complete enough to play), becomes the `wish` frame's `prop` and `choices`, and is drawn by one shared component in all three worlds (`Choice` in `src/film/shots.tsx`). The words of a chosen ending can be changed in the editor; choosing a different kind of ending is done in the short flow. The detailed form does not ask yet.

On `platform_v2` a film's **scenes are its own**. There is no fixed list of scenes to fill ("Red flags", "Reasons", "Memory lane" and "The main character" are gone from films made in the short flow, and from the sample). When the short flow finishes (`POST /api/sort`), a text model reads the note and outlines the film (`src/server/outline.ts`, `gemini-4-neon` unless `GEMINI_OUTLINE_MODEL` says otherwise, 12 to 26 seconds): which scenes it has, what each is called, which of the sender's words go in each, and their order; and, in the same answer, the kind of film and its colours. It is told the occasion, how many photos there are and whether there is a letter; never the photos or the letter.

- **What a scene is.** A `Chapter` (`src/story/types.ts`): an id, a name, a mood, the sender's lines, and one of four layouts, which are the only fixed thing about it. A `line` is one sentence on a screen to itself; a `list` is lines shown in turn; a `moment` is something told (a thing that happened, or a longer thought about who they are) over one or more pages, the first beside a photo and each page after it on a screen of its own (`pagesOf` also turns a long passage over at a sentence's end); `photos` shows the photos no other scene used. The outline is stored as `Intake.outline`; when it is set, `spotlight`, `memories`, `habits` and `reasons` are not used.
- **What the model may and may not write.** It names scenes, and it edits what the sender wrote the way a film editor would (cuts rambling and repetition, mends grammar, shortens for the screen, settles on one way of speaking), because people write and dictate the way they talk. It is told to keep their phrases, quotes and names for things, and to add nothing. Every line is checked for staying close to the sender's words (`closeToTheirWords` in `src/story/notes.ts`, used by `cleanOutline` on the server and again in the browser): endings may change and a few words may be new, but a line built mostly of words they never used is dropped. The start page says the wording is tidied. The rule fallback changes no words. Stock names ("Memories", "Reasons", "Red flags"…) are refused unless the sender used those words; an unnamed scene goes by the opening of its own words. The sender can rename any scene.
- **When the model is off, slow or wrong** the note is outlined by rule (`outlineByWords`): read a sentence at a time, something that happened starts a moment and what carries it on joins it, things that sit together are a list. Its names are plainer, but each moment is still its own scene.
- **How it is drawn.** `generateStory` builds the outline's scenes between the opening and the letter. Each kind of film lists its designs by layout (`worlds.ts`: four ways to draw a list, up to four for a moment), a playful list gets a loud design and a tender one a calm one, and two lists that follow each other never share a design. A single-screen scene carries its own name; only lists get a title card. No flag icon and no "Red flag" or "Reason" label is drawn for these scenes.
- **In the editor** a scene of the film's own shows its name, how it is shown (one line, a list, a moment) and its words, all changeable beside the still, with Undo. "Add a scene" adds one of the sender's own; a scene that is left out or empty can be removed. Moving, leaving out and "Another design" work as before, by the scene's id.
- **The song** is written from the outline (`notesOf`), leaving out scenes the sender left out.
- **Everything is changed in the editor.** The opening scene's panel holds the two names and the occasion; every film has a photos scene (empty until a photo is added) where photos are added, captioned and removed; the letter and the last line are scenes too. Nothing links to the detailed form (`/create`) any more. Its route and the older fixed scenes are still in the code, for gifts stored before outlines and for the tests that build a film that way; removing them is a separate clean-up.
- The studio still says why it chose the kind of film (`Intake.direction`, `directionLine`); for an outlined film the direction carries the kind of film, the colours, the reason, and **the song the director chose**: a tune and a voice (`Direction.tune`, `Direction.voice`). They are used when the sender picked "Surprise me" for the style or the voice (`surpriseVibe`, `surpriseVoice`), and the studio says what it chose while the song is made. The director is told not to guess a voice from a name. Without a model's direction the style is drawn by rule and the voice is left to the music model.

## Changes of 2026-11-20 (later the same day)

- **No ending step.** The short flow no longer asks how the film should end; it ends the way its occasion does (a candle on a birthday, a sealed note otherwise). The engine still plays a stored `Intake.ending` (a question or an invitation), but nothing sets one now.
- **An occasion of the sender's own.** "Something else" on the occasion step (and in the editor's opening scene) takes a few words, stored as `Intake.occasionName`. The film is then built as a "just because" film whose ticket says those words (`occasionFor` in `src/story/occasions.ts`), and the outline and the song are told what it is for.
- **Nothing on the start page explains how the film is made.** The line about an AI model tidying the wording was removed at the owner's request. The song step and the home page's questions still say who writes and sings the song.
- **Clips.** A short video can be added wherever photos are (up to 3 a film, 25 MB and 30 seconds each; MP4, MOV or WebM that the sender's browser can play). It is a `Photo` with a `clip` (the video) beside its `src` (its first frame, drawn on the device by `src/components/clip-intake.ts`). In the film it plays silent and looping where the photo would sit (`src/film/Media.tsx`); stills and thumbnails show the first frame. Nothing is converted, so a browser that can't play a clip shows its first frame instead. On send it is uploaded as `clip:<photo id>`, checked by its first bytes (`sniffVideo`), stored as `<photo id>-clip` and served behind the gift's lock with range requests like the song.
- **A fuller first film.** The outline brief forbids cutting one sentence across two pages. Single-line scenes take a photo each while there are photos no told scene used, so photos sit beside words before any are left for the scene of photos.

## The owner's numbers (2026-11-20)

`/owner` shows how much the studio has made and sent: films, songs, gifts and spoken notes for today, seven days and all time; the last fourteen days; and what sent gifts are made of (kind of film, occasion, song, photos, clips, voice notes). Counts only: nothing a sender wrote, uploaded or recorded is shown. It asks for the owner's key, which is the `OWNER_KEY` environment variable (at least 12 characters; without it nobody is let in), sent as a header to `GET /api/owner` and kept for the browser session only. Gifts are counted from their folders. Films, songs, lyrics and spoken notes are counted from `DATA_DIR/events.log`, one line per event with its time and kind (`record` in `src/server/metrics.ts`), kept since the page was added; nothing before that was counted.

## Voice (2026-11-20)

- **A voice note on a scene.** In the editor any scene of words has "Record a voice note" (up to 45 seconds, `src/components/recorder.ts`). It is kept as `Chapter.voice` (`VoiceNote`: where the sound is and how long it runs). In the film it starts once the scene's title card has gone, is kept in step with the film's own clock (`voiceAt` in `src/film/Film.tsx`), and the song is turned down under it (`AudioEngine.duck`); the scene is held until it has been heard out (`buildFilm`). Pausing, stopping or moving the film stops it. On send it is uploaded as `voice:<scene id>`, checked by its first bytes and stored as `<scene id>-voice` behind the gift's lock.
- **Voice notes while making the film.** The photos step has a voice-notes section: record or upload up to three (`src/components/VoiceNotes.tsx`, kept as `Intake.voices`). When the film is made each one is written down first (`/api/transcribe`), the director is told what each says and puts it on the scene it is about (`voice` on a scene in its answer, carried as `Chapter.voiceId`), and `withVoices` turns that into the scene's `voice`. One the director leaves out goes on the last scene of words that has none, so nothing recorded goes unheard. The director is told not to repeat a voice note's words as lines of the film.
- **Saying it instead of typing it.** The note step has "Say it instead" (up to two minutes). The recording goes to `POST /api/transcribe`, a model writes down what was said (`src/server/transcribe.ts`, with `store: false`), and the words are added to the box for the sender to read and change. The recording is used once and not kept. It needs the Gemini key; without it the button says speaking isn't switched on.

## The home page is one screen (2026-11-20)

Testers found the home page too much, so `/` is now the pitch, the sample film (the Scrapbook, playable in place), one "Start a film" button and three short steps, with nothing to scroll for on a desktop. The occasion tickets, the three kinds of film, the scene stills, the song explainer, "How it works" in four steps and the closing pitch are gone from it; the occasion is chosen inside the flow (a link can still carry one, `/start?occasion=…`). The questions and their answers sit below that first screen, folded shut.

## Privacy of what senders give (2026-11-20)

- **Nothing the studio sends to Google is kept in its request log.** Every request (scenes, follow-up questions, photo placing, lyrics, the song) carries `store: false` (`NOT_STORED` in `src/server/sort.ts`). The project's AI Studio request logging is on; with the flag a request does not appear there, without it it does (checked both ways). Google still holds requests for 55 days for abuse monitoring, and on the paid tier does not use them to improve its models (its terms, read the same day).
- **The director sees the photos.** Small copies (512 px) go with the note when a film is made, and a quick second step puts each scene beside the photo that shows it (`placePhotos` in `src/server/outline.ts`). A scene it leaves out goes without a photo. `DIRECTOR_SEES_PHOTOS=0` turns it off. The photo step says the photos are private; it no longer says they stay on the device.

## Which model does what

Chosen on 2026-11-20 by running the same three notes through each candidate on the test site. Each is one environment variable away from being changed.

| Job | Model | Why | Tried and not chosen |
|---|---|---|---|
| A film's scenes (`outline.ts`) | `gemini-4-neon`, low thinking | Followed the brief most exactly: kept the closing line out of the scenes, named the photo scene, steady moods. 12 to 26 s across two runs. | `gemini-3.8-flash` (5 s; looser: closing line filed as a scene, "Photos", mixed capitals), `gemini-4-hydrogen` (10 s; same slips) |
| Follow-up questions (`followups.ts`) | `gemini-3.8-flash`, low thinking | The sender is waiting on the step. 3.5 s, questions as good as the bigger models'. | `gemini-4-hydrogen` (7 to 9 s, no better), `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (2.3 s; fewer and clumsier questions) |
| Lyrics (`lyrics.ts`) | `gemini-4-neon`, low thinking | Songs that allude instead of quoting, and nothing invented. 25 to 30 s. | `gemini-4-carbon` (8 to 25 s, the most vivid, but added props that were never said), Neon at medium thinking (a little better, 50 to 60 s) |
| The song (`music.ts`) | `lyria-3.5` | Google's newest music model; nothing newer is listed. | |

Text for one film costs a few cents at Neon's price ($3 in, $15 out per million tokens); a song is about $0.08. Neither figure has been checked against a bill.

## Music

A film's only music is the song written and sung for it. There is no built-in or studio music anywhere: none can be chosen, none plays while a song is being made, and none stands in when a song fails. The synth score that used to do those things has been removed from the engine, the generator, the story format and the audio code (stored test gifts that named score tracks simply play without them).

- While its song is being made, the film is held at its opening (the ticket or the album cover, dimmed, with "It starts when their song is ready") and cannot be started. The sender can change scenes and style meanwhile.
- When the song arrives it goes onto the film by itself and the film can be played.
- If the song fails, or songs are switched off in this studio, the film plays in silence, the credits carry no Music line, and the send page says the film has no song.
- A song is never asked for twice at once (`inFlight` in `src/components/draft.tsx`).
- Google's music model refuses lyrics that come too close to an existing song (seen live on a thank-you song for a friend). When that happens the words are written again, with a note to the writer to keep clear of song titles and famous lines, and sung again, up to three times, before the sender is told anything.

## The film (`src/film/`)

The gift is a film that plays by itself, not a slideshow.

- `timeline.ts`: turns a story into timed shots. Lengths come from reading time, snapped to whole bars of the scene's music so cuts land on the beat. Plain data, so it can later drive an MP4 render.
- `Film.tsx`: the player. Tapping the ticket tears it, a film leader counts 3-2-1, then a clock runs the shots. The film waits at the candle (or, for anything but a birthday, the sealed note) and at the end. There is a player bar (back, pause, next, scrubber with scene marks, sound, full screen) and keyboard control (Space, arrows, M, F). It pauses when the tab is hidden.
- `shots.tsx`: what each shot looks like: lines typed letter by letter, the name drawn from dust, scene cards, framed photos with a slow push, flat-colour red flags that slam in on the beat, prints dealt for the camera roll, a letter that scrolls as it is read, the candle or the sealed note, the last line and rolling credits.
- `backdrop.ts`: WebGL smoke behind everything. It drifts between palettes per mood. Falls back to a CSS gradient.
- `particles.ts`: dust, words that gather from or scatter into dust, candle sparks and confetti.
- `scrapbook.tsx` and `src/styles/scrapbook.css`: the Scrapbook. The same shots drawn as pages of an album: a closed book that opens, one title page, chapter pages with a band of tape, prints with tape, corners and clips, handwriting (Caveat) and typed slips (Courier Prime, both under the Open Font License in `public/fonts`). The page turns between shots; a list (sticky notes, checklist, tags, cards) builds on the page it is on. No smoke, dust or letterbox bars.
- `mixtape.tsx` and `src/styles/mixtape.css`: the Mixtape. The same shots drawn as tracks on a tape: a cassette with their name on its label and "Press play", one title track, a numbered card with level meters between scenes, photos as cover art or as a poster that runs off the frame, red flags as loud tracks (one to a screen, on changing colour) or a track listing that builds, reasons as "now playing" with a line that runs for the length of the shot or as liner notes, the camera roll as a rack of covers, and the letter typed on the unfolded inlay card. Type is the site's sans at its narrowest and heaviest, in capitals; the small print is Courier Prime. Its three colourways are Midnight, Sunset and Daylight. `useShrink` steps a track's type down until its words fit, measured from layout boxes so nothing still sliding in counts.
- `looks.ts`: the three colourways of each world as palettes.
- Reduced motion: no countdown, particles or smoke movement; shots still change with simple fades.
- Styles: `src/styles/film.css` (sized in container units, so the film scales to its box) and `src/styles/room.css` (the studio's working pages: a pale mist field with white cards over a bright wall, the same language as the home page).

## Server

- `src/routes/api/*`: plain Request/Response handlers (create, unlock, gift, media, manage, music, song).
- `src/server/store.ts`: one folder per gift under `DATA_DIR` (`gift.json` and `media/`). It can be swapped for Postgres and R2 later behind the same functions.
- `src/story/validate.ts`: server-side limits on every intake field. The server rebuilds the story from the intake and seed, so it never trusts a story sent by the browser.
- `src/server/music.ts`: Lyria 3.5 through the Gemini Interactions API (preferred), or legacy Lyria 3 Pro through OpenRouter (Lyria 3.5 isn't on OpenRouter yet). Songs carry Google's inaudible SynthID watermark. Sung prompts (`sungPrompt` in `src/story/vibes.ts`) carry the recipient's and sender's names and up to three reasons, three habits, two memories and the wish, cleaned and capped on the server; the letter and photos are never sent. Instrumental prompts carry nothing personal. When the request carries `lyrics` (the normal path), `lyricsPrompt` asks for the song as a recipe in the order Google's Lyria guidance recommends: genre and tempo, instruments, mood, the vocalist (`voice`: a man's, a woman's, or left open), production, structure, then the lyrics to sing exactly. This is the shape that produced the sample's song, and the Afrobeats style is that song's recipe. The other four styles were rewritten into the same shape and have not been listened to in it. In the one real comparison made with the earlier prompt, all 22 lines came back word for word with nothing added. The approved words and the song's title are stored with it. Google refuses a request whose lyrics quote an existing song (HTTP 400, code `recitation`); the line "Happy birthday to you" does this, so it is caught in the page, in the endpoint (422) and in the writer's output. There are per-client and daily caps. `MUSIC_PROVIDER=demo` makes short local tones for tests.
- `src/components/audio-engine.ts`: a song is streamed through a media element wired into Web Audio, so it starts as soon as the first second has arrived (measured at 1.5 Mbit/s: the old download-then-decode left about 12 seconds of silence after the film began; streaming starts with the film). The element is played once, muted, inside the tap so gesture-only browsers allow it later; if streaming is refused, the whole file is decoded as before. Songs are served with byte ranges (`mediaResponse` in `src/server/store.ts`).
- `src/server/lyrics.ts`: writes the words with `gemini-4-neon` (`GEMINI_LYRICS_MODEL`), low thinking (`GEMINI_LYRICS_THINKING`), JSON output, about 25 to 30 seconds. The brief (`lyricsBrief` in `src/story/vibes.ts`) has two tones, chosen in the song’s room. `smooth` (the default) writes a spare track on one hook from the sender's own phrase, uses at most three details without explaining them, and keeps the name and "happy birthday" for the bridge and outro. `story` tells one moment in order, teases with the punchline last and puts the birthday in the chorus. Each carries a worked example about two invented friends, kept free of any one country's references. For other occasions the same brief swaps in that occasion's greeting (or none, for "just because") and adds a line saying the worked example is a birthday song and this one is not. Only the birthday brief has been tuned by ear; for the other occasions one set of lyrics each was written and read for anniversary, just because and missing you (right greeting, in the bridge and outro only, no birthday wording), but no song has been made or heard for them. In side-by-side reads, Flash at the same speed invented details to reach rhymes, and higher thinking levels took two minutes without writing better. Same key as the music. Caps: `LYRICS_HOURLY_PER_CLIENT` (20) and `LYRICS_DAILY_LIMIT` (300). `MUSIC_PROVIDER=demo` returns placeholder words.
- Production needs `SESSION_SECRET` (32+ characters). Without it, the server refuses to unlock gifts.
